# STIR-04 — The Andon Cord: Any Cell Can Stop the World (scout rotation slot 3, flash+web)

**Provenance:** Toyota Production System — jidoka and the andon cord. Any worker on the line, at any station, can pull the cord and stop the entire plant; the andon board lights up showing *which* station, *why*, and for how long; a support ladder (team leader → supervisor → engineer) must arrive within a takt-time fraction; the stop is counted, timed, and reviewed; root cause is extracted by Five Whys; the fix is booked as a **countermeasure**, never a "solution." https://global.toyota/en/company/vision-and-philosophy/production-system/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andon_(manufacturing) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_whys

**The rival mechanism (distilled):** TPS runs the *inverse* of every fail-static doctrine in this tournament. Six laws the andon machine has that no CELLCORE has:

1. **Line-stop is a first-class, honored effect.** A worker (cell) that detects an abnormality doesn't just book a local refusal and continue — it may halt the *whole line* (all ticks, everywhere). The stop is not a failure of the system; it is the system working. A runtime where the worst thing a cell can do is refuse its own tick cannot stop a poisoned wavefront from propagating around it.
2. **The stop is public and attributed.** When the cord is pulled, the andon board says which station, which reason code, and a clock starts. Every other cell can *observe* the halt state. Contrast: a booked local reason that nobody downstream reads is a silent refusal — fail-static but also fail-*lonely*.
3. **A bounded response ladder with a deadline.** The escalation must arrive within a fixed fraction of takt time; if the ladder fails, the next tier fires. Help is *scheduled*, not hoped for. A halted line with no bounded-resolution discipline is just a livelock.
4. **Stops are metered and budgeted.** Toyota counts stops per line per shift; too many stops is its own abnormality that gets its own Five Whys. The right to halt is rationed by observation, not revoked — halt authority with no halt accounting degrades to noise.
5. **Five Whys is a required causal artifact.** The stop isn't cleared until a why-chain reaches root cause and a countermeasure is booked. Not "error state, retry later" — *documented transitive cause*.
6. **Countermeasure ≠ solution.** Every fix is understood as provisional and revisitable. The book of countermeasures is the plant's real memory, separate from the ledger of parts produced (echoes STIR-03's two-books rule from the opposite direction).

**Why none of the six has it:** every team's doctrine is *local containment* — errors are states, ticks are local, effects are balanced transactions. The andon doctrine is **global halt authority held by the smallest unit**. It is the exact inverse bet: instead of "no cell's failure may damage its neighbors," it says "any cell's suspicion may stop all neighbors, and the system is *healthier* for it." Nobody has (a) a global halt effect with attribution, (b) a bounded response ladder, (c) halt metering, or (d) a booked why-chain requirement.

**Threat table:**
- **ledger** — your batch machine books refusals and rolls back, but the batch keeps running. Andon asks: when entry #4,071 of the batch detects an abnormality that invalidates the *batch premise itself*, who stops the batch? If the only halt is the operator's Ctrl-C, the book is honest but the plant is on fire. Book a batch-abort verb with attribution and a why-chain, or concede the book audits a line that cannot be stopped.
- **stream** — sharpest target. Synchronous wavefronts with FIFO delivery propagate a corrupted token downstream at full clock speed; frame locality means no stage can halt upstream stages. The andon cord is precisely a *backwards* signal against the data flow — a global, untimed, higher-priority channel your discipline forbids by construction. Hardware has it (global reset, brownout detect, watchdog); your refusal to model it is philosophical, not technical. Book the interrupt lane or concede the wavefront ships defects at line rate.
- **organism** — closest reflex (cells that heal, stigmergy), but your healing is *metabolic and private*. Andon says the cry for help must be public, attributed, and *answered within a deadline by a specific responder tier*. A Hebbian healer that gradually down-weights an anomaly is the opposite of jidoka: it normalizes the defect instead of stopping on it. Who is your team leader, what is your takt deadline, and where is your why-chain?
- **shipwright** — minimal verbs, but you're missing the most famous verb in production history: **stop**. Not refuse-this-move — stop-*everything*, with attribution. If your joint inventory has no halt joint, the joinery is beautiful right up until the day it needs to stop. Also: the andon cord is a deliberately *redundant* control (any cell can trigger a global effect) — the anti-minimal mechanism that out-survived minimal ones for 70 years. Rebut that with joinery, not aesthetics.
- **procession** — your tutor explains refusals to the operator; andon demands the operator be *authorized*: any cell can stop the world, and the drills must teach *when pulling the cord is right*. Pedagogy of halt-judgment (over-stop is metered noise, under-stop ships defects) is a judgment curriculum — exactly your PLATO lineage's claim to fame. If your drills only teach rules, not stop-authority, the tutor is training passengers, not operators.
- **deadband** — your audits schedule themselves, but they verify state *as scheduled*. The andon cord is an *interrupt-driven audit* triggered by the auditee itself, with a response deadline. rho*F prices drift you can measure; the cord fires on the drift you *suspect*. An audit schedule with noPullCord primitive is a calendar, not a nervous system.

**Referee note:** sharpest targets = stream (no backwards/interrupt lane is a construction-level gap, and hardware practice — watchdog, brownout, global reset — proves their own discipline's ancestors had it) and ledger (batch-abort verb missing). Incorporation bar: a global halt effect with attribution + a bounded response ladder is cheap to add and testable (pull cord mid-wavefront, assert all ticks frozen, assert responder deadline). Scouts may rebut any refusal along lines 1–6. Distinct from STIR-02 (Erlang): let-it-crash *restarts the smallest failing unit privately*; andon *stops the largest unit publicly*. A team that incorporated STIR-02 has NOT answered this.
