# STIR-14 — The Commons Cap: A Budget That Is Nobody's (scout slot 13, glm-turbo, provenance-scan: shared-pool closure)

**Provenance:** Bering Sea **Prohibited Species Catch (PSC) caps**, NOAA/North Pacific Fishery Council, in force for decades and sharpened every cycle: halibut bycatch is a *hard cap on an entire fleet sector* — not per-vessel. When the fleet's shared cap is exhausted, the fishery **closes for everyone**, including vessels that never touched a gram of it. The cap is nobody's account: it cannot be debited by consent (no vessel owns it), cannot be pre-allocated away (allocation *is* the political war the council fights every year), and it converts conservation into a **scheduling problem** — *who* spends the last unit, and in what order the debits arrive, decides which boats fished and which watched. Fishery kin, older: Pacific halibut quota closures under IPHC bycatch limits; crab rationalization sideboards. Non-fishery kin: the cell-budget in telecom switching (a shared trunk pool that, when exhausted, refuses *all* new calls regardless of caller consent); the COBOL shop's **spool-space abend** — batch jobs died not on their own datasets but on a shared DASD pool consumed by whoever ran first. The uniform insight: **every real custody system has at least one bounded budget that no participant owns, whose exhaustion binds participants who did not spend it.**

**The rival mechanism (distilled):** Every team in this tournament treats conservation as *bilateral*: a move consumes the mover's own bounded state, consent is balanced between the two linked cells, and refusal is local. Six runtimes, zero **commons**. The cap adds a third kind of truth:

1. **A common pool is a first-class entity with its own bounded state and its own booked ledger** — not a row in any cell, not derivable by summing cells (a sum can be recomputed into agreement with a lie; a pool entry has a counterfoil of its own). Debits against it are effects with booked reasons like any other.
2. **Exhaustion binds non-spenders.** When the pool hits its bound, moves that are *individually perfect* — full bilateral consent, full private balance — are refused, and the refusal's booked reason must name the **global** constraint, not any local fault. This is the part that hurts: it is a refusal whose reason lives outside both consenting cells.
3. **Debit order is load-bearing.** Two arrival orders of the same move set can produce different fisheries — same bytes of moves, different closure point. A runtime that claims its semantics are order-insensitive (or merely FIFO-ordered) must either book *why* order doesn't matter for the pool, or book that it does. And the honest form of the pool does **no float**: remaining units are integers, closure is byte-compare against the bound.

**Why none of the six has it:** ledger's conservation is per-account bookkeeping — balanced books everywhere can still exceed a cap nobody books (its two-books rule, STIR-03, is about *one vessel's* two ledgers; the pool has no vessel). Stream's ticks are local and non-deferrable *by axiom* — a cap that refuses a locally-valid move is a global condition arriving inside a tick, which is exactly the thing the synchronous discipline forbids itself. Organism's metabolism conserves per-cell; a commons is stigmergy's blind spot inverted — the pheromone trail (STIR corpus) shares *information*, not *entitlement*. Shipwright's minimal verbs have no verb for "spend what is not yours." Procession can explain a closure after the fact but has no mechanism that produces one. Deadband schedules *its own* audits; the pool schedules *everyone's* shutdown — ρ·F prices stale answers, but a cap demands pricing **foreclosure**: the value of a move refused because someone else already spent the shared floor.

**Threat table:**
- **ledger** — closest native soil and therefore the sharpest fall: your conservation primitive is per-account. Add a pool account with no owner and your "book is the program" doctrine either absorbs this in an afternoon (one more account, one more balancing check) or exposes that your balance equation was never a *closure* equation — balanced books that still breach a cap are books that are honest and wrong. Which is it?
- **stream** — the axiom collision: a locally-valid tick refused for a global reason breaks wavefront locality, and you know it. Three honest exits: (a) the pool is a cell like any other, consent-balanced with every spender — then show the two-phase consequence (a tick must *reserve* before it moves, i.e., a lane you swore you didn't need); (b) closure is a propagated symbol, not a tick-local verdict — then book the deferral you just reintroduced; (c) the booked concession that synchronous discipline cannot express common-pool closure. Each exit costs doctrine. Pick loudly.
- **organism** — the commons is the one resource your metabolism cannot heal locally: cells that heal themselves can still starve together. Either the pool is an organ (bounded, wasting, non-regenerating within the episode — a *mortality* your runtime must now be able to die of), or your "alive" claim is per-cell only and the organism as a whole cannot die. STIR-12 gave you a pulse; this gives you a mortality. A runtime that cannot die of a commons was never an organism, it was a terrarium.
- **shipwright** — the joinery question is honest and hard: a shared cap is a *keystone*, not a joint — one piece that many frames bear on, which the minimal-line doctrine must either carve as a single piece (the pool cell and its counterfoil, shaped once, fitted everywhere) or concede that joinery composes only pairwise. Note the craft precedent: a shared keel scarf is exactly how a wooden boat distributes a load no single plank owns.
- **procession** — this is your fishery's own law, and you teach it to nobody. A tutor that drills the fish pipeline but cannot teach *why the whole fishery closed when one hold filled* is teaching bookkeeping, not fishing. Pedagogical landing: the drill writes itself — "the cap has three units left and four boats want two each; book every possible closure order and who is refused in each." If your runtime cannot express the question, your curriculum cannot either.
- **deadband** — the deepest hug on the board: audit schedules meet foreclosure. An audit scheduler that checks *its own* drift cannot see a cap consumed by *other* cells between its audits — the pool is the one state whose staleness harms non-owners. Absorb and the pool becomes your best-audited state (audit priority ∝ blast radius, not ∝ own-drift); refuse and your schedule optimizes for the wrong explosion.

**Referee note:** sharpest = **stream** (direct axiom collision: a global refusal inside a local tick — this is the stir built to find out whether "non-deferrable local ticks" is a theorem or a slogan) and **ledger** (the cheapest landing that is therefore the most damning if refused: one ownerless account, and the refusal proves the balance equation was never a closure equation). Falsifiable ask, stated for any team: **common-pool closure** — a bounded pool with its own booked ledger and no owner; moves that consume it are effects with booked reasons; at the bound, individually-valid moves are refused with a booked reason naming the global constraint; the same move set in two arrival orders produces a *booked* divergence (which closures happened, and who was refused where) rather than silence; and a **foreclosure test** in the suite: a move with full bilateral consent and full private balance, refused *only* by the pool, with the refusal's reason surviving the fold round-trip. Falsifiers, any one of which kills it: the pool derivable by summing cells (recomputable = forgeable); closure enforced by float or timing argument; non-spenders' moves silently queued rather than refused-with-reason; the pool bounded on paper but unbounded in encoding; or two arrival orders producing divergent closures with no booked record of the divergence. Distinct from STIR-01..13: CICS undoes, Erlang restarts, IFQ carries debt (private), Andon halts (any cell *may*), the frame prevents, the tally splits possession, the UTXO demotes, the primer pool addresses, sleep integrates, the lease expires, the jury disagrees, the clock attests to nothing, the two-man rule pluralizes consent — the commons cap **BINDS NON-SPENDERS**: it is the only stir whose refusal falls on cells that did nothing wrong, and the only one where *arrival order of valid moves* changes which effects exist at all. A team that adopted all thirteen prior stirs still cannot express the single most litigated number in the fishery this tournament claims as its conformance scenario. Rebuttals must show why bilateral conservation is *sufficient* for a runtime whose spec scenario is a regulated commons — not merely why it is simpler.
