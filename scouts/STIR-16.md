# STIR-16 — The Pilot: Borrowed Authority, Retained Accountability (scout slot 15, flash+web)

**Provenance:** Maritime pilotage, one of the oldest formal delegation systems afloat. For centuries, entering a hazardous port required taking aboard a local pilot — a specialist who commands the ship's movements through waters the master doesn't know (Sandy Hook pilots, est. 1694; Puget Sound & Alaska marine pilots, state-mandated today for tankers and foreign flag in Prince William Sound — the waters Casey's family fishes). The legal shape is the mechanism, and it is peculiar: **the pilot has con (conduct of navigation) but the master retains command.** The pilot's orders to the helm are executed; the master may countermand them at any moment, and the master — not the pilot — answers for the grounding. Two more load-bearing details: (a) the pilot's mandate is **geographically and temporally bounded** — pilotage waters are charted in advance, and the pilot leaves at the sea buoy; authority is issued for a *passage*, not in general; (b) the passage is **booked before and after** — embarkation/debarkation entered in the deck log, so any maneuver in the interval is attributable: under pilot, or master's override. Sources: maritime pilotage law & practice (master-pilot relationship, "the master retains command"), 46 U.S.C. §8502 (mandatory pilotage), Prince William Sound / Puget Sound pilot regimes (RCW 88.16; Alyeska/POVTS escort-and-pilot system post-Exxon Valdez 1989, where the failure was a pilot-conducted passage with the master absent from the bridge — the canonical incident). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_pilot ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exxon_Valdez_oil_spill

**The rival mechanism (distilled):** Every CELLCORE in this tournament runs with one mind: the core's own rules decide every tick, in every state, forever. Pilotage is a runtime that **injects a foreign, superior decider for a declared hazardous region and a declared interval, with mandatory handover, while accountability never moves.** Four laws:

1. **Con without command.** In pilotage waters, the injected authority's directives ARE executed by the runtime — not advisory text, actual steering — but the master's override is always one booked move away, and the override does not require the pilot's consent. Verdict class: two live authorities with unequal precedence where the LOWER one can always overrule the higher at booked cost.
2. **The mandate is a region and an interval, charted in peacetime.** You declare *which* states of the system constitute hazardous waters (fold storms, cold start, recovery from a corrupted counterfoil, the seam between epochs) and the pilot is *mandatory there and forbidden elsewhere*. Not a supervisor always on, not a fallback always armed: an authority that exists exactly on the chart and no wider — taking it where it isn't chartered is a violation, same as refusing it where it is.
3. **Embarkation is booked or the passage doesn't count.** The interval of delegated con is delimited in the log: every effect inside the interval carries the pilot's stamp (which pilot, which chart version); every effect after debarkation that still wears the stamp is refused — stale authority, byte-compare, no timing argument. Handover is a first-class state transition, not an error, not a restart (STIR-02): the state is continuous, the *authority* is what moves.
4. **The grounding is the master's.** Delegation never transfers accountability. A runtime that ran a fold storm under an injected pilot and lost data still books the fault against ITSELF, with the pilot's contribution as evidence, not as defendant. No team can land "the imported decider did it."

**Why none of the six has it:** every team's deciders are either native (the core's own rules — all six) or external-but-either-or (STIR-11's dissimilar jury replaces judgment with consensus; STIR-05's backstop prevents an escape). Nobody has **hierarchical con with subordinate override**, a **charted region-of-authority** the runtime itself enforces in both directions (mandatory inside, forbidden outside), **stamped effects with interval-bounded validity** (STIR-10's lease expires *authority*; the pilot stamp validates *effects* retroactively-by-interval — different object), and **non-transferable accountability** (STIR-12 attests but doesn't steer; STIR-14 binds but doesn't delegate).
