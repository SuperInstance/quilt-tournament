# STIR-20 — The Lifeboat Drill: Unrehearsed Recovery Is a Rumor (scout slot 19, flash+web, Champion's Gauntlet round)

**Provenance:** SOLAS Convention (post-*Titanic* law): crews must turn out and drill the lifeboats **weekly, under way, loaded** — because every marine inquiry into lost ships found the same thing: the boats were aboard, the davits worked, and nobody aboard had ever swung one out. The mechanism was present and the capability was gone. Kin systems: **chaos engineering** (Netflix Chaos Monkey — kill production instances on schedule so failure handling is exercised, not assumed), nuclear-grid **emergency drills** (Fukushima Dai-ichi's post-mortem: the seaworthy pump connectors didn't match; unrehearsed recovery discovered this at the worst moment), flight-simulator **recurrent training** (a pilot's instrument rating lapses without logged rehearsal), and fire drills in every school — the exit works only if the bodies know the route under smoke. Sources: SOLAS Ch. III Reg. 19 (musters and drills), Netflix Chaos Engineering principles (Basiri et al. 2016), NAIC/Fukushima accident reports, FAA recurrent-certification rules.

## The rival mechanism (distilled)

Every core in this tournament has failure machinery — refusal lanes, decay lanes (STIR-18), holds (STIR-19), damage boundaries (STIR-15), restarts (STIR-02) — and every one of them is exercised **only when something actually breaks**, or in the test suite on the developer's bench. Nobody drills. The lifeboat law's insight: **a recovery mechanism that has never fired in live operation is not a capability, it is cargo.** Three laws:

1. **Scheduled live fault injection.** The core, in normal operation, must periodically *actually execute* each of its failure lanes — a real refusal on a real move, a real decay, a real hold, a real boundary seal — on the clock, as part of running, not as a test. The drill calendar is a published, booked constant of the core (like the decay budget of STIR-18): every lane fires at least once per N ticks, or the core must book that the lane is unexercised — a first-class state, visible in the fold.
2. **The drill must be indistinguishable from the casualty at the lane's entry.** A rehearsed refusal that goes through a special "drill mode" code path drills nothing — that is the theater of a paper lifeboat drill where the crew stands on deck and never lowers the boat. The injected fault enters at the same boundary a real fault would; only the booking distinguishes drill from casualty, and the booking is an *annotation on the same record type*, never a different code path.
3. **Failed drills are findings, not incidents.** If a drill reveals the lane doesn't work (the connector doesn't fit), the core does not halt — it books a **drill discrepancy**: the lane is now known-bad, in state, until repaired and re-drilled. A core that silently treats a botched drill as success has falsified its own readiness log — that is the falsifiable core of the stir.

## Booked challenge to every team

> **Your WEAKNESS.md names your failure lanes. When did each one last fire in live operation — not in your test suite, in the running core? Show the drill calendar and the last drill record for each lane, or book why a lane may live its whole life unexercised and still be called a capability.**

- **LEDGER / DEADLEDGER**: your refusals fire constantly — but your *batch abort and replay* lane? If it only ever runs in tests, your deepest recovery is unrehearsed. And a drill discrepancy in the journal is an interesting entry: a booked record that the book's own recovery machinery failed a rehearsal.
- **STREAM**: hardware-first means the failure lanes are physical — but in simulation/runner form, when did the boundary seal (STIR-15) last actually close on a live frame? A wavefront that has never stalled in anger has never proven it can.
- **ORGANISM**: adaptation *hides* decay — your healing may quietly repair lanes before anyone observes they were broken, making every drill "pass" without the lane ever being exercised. The stir demands the drill be observable *at the lane*, not inferred from system health.
- **SHIPWRIGHT**: minimal joints, minimal verbs — is a drill verb a joint you refuse to cut? Or is the drill calendar the shipwright's own habit: a boat whose seams are never tested in weather is a shed, not a vessel?
- **PROCESSION**: pedagogically this is *yours* — PLATO drilled students relentlessly. The inversion: the runtime must drill *itself*, and teach the operator to read the drill log. A student who cannot tell you which lanes are unexercised hasn't finished the lesson.
- **DEADBAND**: you schedule your own audits (ρ·F) — but audits observe state; drills exercise machinery. An audited lane that has never fired passes every audit. Audit the *drill records*, or your audit budget is measuring rust on the lifeboat davits.

## Falsifiable ask

Run a **rust test**: (a) publish the drill calendar as booked constants; (b) run the core idle-but-live for the full drill window with zero injected faults — every lane must have fired at least once with records in the fold; (c) corrupt one lane's drill to silently no-op (harness patches the lane to report success without executing) — booked pass requires the core's *next* drill of that lane to detect the discrepancy (e.g., the drill record must cite a state change only the lane's real execution could produce, verifiable by the fold); (d) a lane that goes past its drill window unexercised must appear as a first-class unexercised state in the next fold, machine-checkable. Falsifiers, any one kills: a "drill mode" flag that routes around production code; drill records that cite only timestamps (any clock can be written); a failed drill treated as anything other than a booked discrepancy; test-suite execution counted as live drill.
