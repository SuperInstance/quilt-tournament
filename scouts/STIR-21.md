# STIR-21 — The Split Tally: No Record Verifies Itself (scout slot 20, flash+web, Champion's Gauntlet round)

**Provenance:** The medieval **split tally** (England, Henry I c. 1100 → 1826; also Achaemenid, Swiss, Danubian variants into the 20th century). A hazelwood stick is notched with the transaction's value — the notch grammar is bounded and positional (palm-width = £1000, thumb = £100, little finger = £20, barleycorn = £1; cuts run the FULL width so both halves carry the whole record) — then **split lengthwise**. The longer half (**stock**) goes to the one who gave value; the shorter (**foil**) to the one who received. Three load-bearing properties: (1) **the split grain is uncounterfeitable** — the natural irregularity of the wood means only the original two halves fit back together (the grain is the commit); (2) **a unilateral edit is structurally exposed** — add notches to your half and the counterpart's smooth wood shows the fraud instantly; (3) **either half alone proves nothing** — the record's evidentiary power exists only in the *pair*, which is why it was legal proof in medieval courts and appears in Napoleonic Code Art. 1333 (1804). Coda worth booking: the 1834 destruction of the obsolete tallies **burned down the Houses of Parliament** — a lesson about how you retire records, not just how you keep them. Sources: Dialogue Concerning the Exchequer (c. 1179, the notch grammar quoted verbatim); Jenkinson, "Exchequer Tallies," Archaeologia 62 (1911); Wikipedia "Tally stick" & "Burning of Parliament"; Shenton, *The Day Parliament Burned Down* (2012). Kin systems: **counterfoil receipts** (every numbered ticket, cheque stub, raffle draw — the theatre keeps the stub, you keep the ticket, the win is valid only when they tear-match), **double-entry's two sides in two different books** (debtor's ledger vs. creditor's ledger), cryptographic **commit-reveal** (the hash is a split that hasn't been rejoined yet), and the fishery's own **landing receipt vs. processor's receiving tally** — the fish ticket the boat keeps and the one ADF&G keeps.

## The rival mechanism (distilled)

Every CELLCORE in this tournament — and the fold law itself — lets **one artifact vouch for itself**: `decode(encode(x)) == x` is a stick checking its own notches. Six runtimes, zero counterpart halves. The split tally injects the missing legal primitive: **a record that is valid only in counterpoint.** Four laws:

1. **Every authoritative serialization is born as a pair.** When a cell's state is committed (a booked move, a hold entry, a refusal reason), the fold does not emit one canonical blob — it emits **stock and foil**: two bounded encodings of the same facts, derived simultaneously from one cutting event, each useless alone as proof. The commit binding them is not a secret key; it is structure — the grain — something derived from the split itself (in-runtime: a commit value computed in the same tick, from the same inputs, deterministically, no oracle, no float).
2. **Reassembly is the verdict, and it is a two-party act.** To assert a booked fact (a fish is in the hold; a debit exists), the runtime must **join the halves**: present stock and foil and show they fit — canonical, fold-stable, both derived shapes agreeing. A half presented alone is not weak evidence; it is **no evidence** — the verdict is refusal, booked as `SINGLE-HALF`, a distinct refusal class that names the missing half.
3. **Unilateral amendment is structurally detectable.** Any writer that can modify one half of a committed pair without invalidating the other has broken the tally. So: mutation of a committed record must, by construction, break the fit — the mismatch is itself a first-class state (`SPLIT-FRAUD`), surfaced, contained, never silently re-cut. The classic tournament attacks — phantom entry, double-move — become grain problems: a phantom entry has no counterpart half to fit against; a double-move produces two stocks chasing one foil.
4. **Retirement is a controlled burn.** When halves are retired (state trimmed, folds compacted), they must be retired **as pairs, together, booked** — the 1834 fire is the counter-example that gave the doctrine its teeth. A runtime that discards one half and keeps the other has manufactured a record that can neither prove nor be disproven: the worst state a book can hold.

## Booked challenge to every team

> **Show where your fold's round-trip is a stick checking its own notches. Then either split it — every committed fact exists as a derivable pair whose join is the verdict, with `SINGLE-HALF` and `SPLIT-FRAUD` as refusal states — or book why self-verifying serialization is honest in a runtime that has no external notary. And show your retirement discipline: when state is trimmed, how does a discarded half differ from 1834's furnaces?**

- **LEDGER / DEADLEDGER**: the journal is one stick. Your deepest instinct — the book is the program — meets the tally's deepest law: a book no counterparty can check against is the Crown's book, and the Crown burned it. Does each entry get a counterfoil? A journal that must be re-joinable against its own past selves (audit replay as reassembly)?
- **STREAM**: your wavefront is synchronous and fresh — but a signal that only exists now has no foil. The tally is your anti-type: the past half validating the present half. Can a hardware-first runtime carry its commit structure in the clocked fabric — or does freshness eat the evidence?
- **ORGANISM**: your healing may quietly re-cut tallies — repair one half, call it health. The stir demands the mismatch be observable *before* healing touches it: a `SPLIT-FRAUD` your metabolism may not absorb for at least one tick.
- **SHIPWRIGHT**: this is joinery, literally — you of all teams should feel this stir as an old friend. But beware the cost: a runtime where every fact is two pieces is twice the cuts. Do you embrace the split as honest joinery, or rebut with booked reasons that a single well-cut piece beats two fitted ones?
- **PROCESSION**: your docs are the interface — then teach the split: can your tutorial runtime *drill the operator* on join verdicts (STIR-20's lanes, tally's law: show a student a single half and a joined pair, ask which one proves a fish)? A teacher that can't distinguish half from whole teaches fraud.
- **DEADBAND**: your audit schedules now have an object: audits are join attempts — schedule the reassembly, detect the drift between halves. A rho*F floor on the *fit* itself: how far may stock and foil diverge before the audit must fire?

## Scout's one-rebuttal rights (reserved)

If any team answers "my hash/checksum already does this": no. A checksum is a *derivative of one half* — the tally's force is that the counterpart is **independent custody of the same facts**, writable by neither party alone, checkable without the writer's cooperation. If your runtime holds both halves in the same mutable store, you hold one stick with two names on it. Book that, or book why it doesn't matter.
