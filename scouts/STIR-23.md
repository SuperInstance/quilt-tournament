# STIR-23 — The Kanban Hook: The Sender Spends What the Receiver Grants (scout slot 22, glm-5.2, Champion's Gauntlet round)

**Provenance:** **Toyota's kanban system** (Taiichi Ohno, *Toyota Production System*, 1988; Sugimori et al., "Toyota production system and kanban system," IJPR 1977): the cardinal law is that **upstream may produce only on receipt of a production-authorization card withdrawn by downstream consumption** — work-in-progress is capped not by policy but by the physical count of cards in circulation; when the cards are gone, the line upstream stops, by construction, with no supervisor consulted. The same mechanism, older and closer to home: **credit-based flow control in SNA session pacing and ATM** (the receiver grants a window of units; the sender's channel literally cannot represent more outstanding units than granted — the grant is bounded integer arithmetic, no float, no timers), TCP receive windows, and — the tournament's own water — **the setline: 30 hooks at 1.5 fathoms is a kanban of 30.** A hook with no slot free cannot accept a fish; the gear's capacity IS the authorization. Cannery kin: the tender waits on the offload because the receiving bin is full — flow stops at the receiver's absorption rate, and the tender's full hold is a *booked consequence of the stop*, never a silent overflow. Sources: Ohno (1988); Sugimori et al. (1977); IBM SNA session pacing (VTAM pacing window, integer credit); ATM ABR credit-based flow control (ITU-T I.371).

**The rival mechanism (distilled):** Every CELLCORE in this tournament treats refusal as the *receiver's* verdict at move time: a move arrives, the receiver checks its own bounds, books or refuses. Six runtimes, zero **sender-side meters**. The kanban hook inverts the default: **the right to send is a spendable grant issued by the receiver, and when the grant is spent, the send is unrepresentable — not refused.** Four laws:

1. **Capacity is issued, never polled.** Each link carries a bounded integer credit counter, decremented by the sender *at emit time* (before the effect exists), incremented only by the receiver's booked consumption (fish landed, tote emptied, fold accepted). A cell with zero credit on a link cannot emit into it — the request grammar itself has no move for "send without credit," which is stronger than a refused move (compare STIR-22's unrepresentable-by-possession; here what's unrepresentable is *exceeding the receiver's granted absorption*, not lacking a token to a place). No float, no clocks: the counter is the whole mechanism.
2. **The stop is the sender's state, and it round-trips.** A spent credit is part of the sender's bounded state — so the fold must encode it (a cell that has spent all grants is *not* canonically identical to a fresh one, even if every other byte matches). Adversarial duty sharpens: a replayed or forged move from before the spend must fail on the counter, not on a state check the forger can race. Truncated fold that restores credit the sender never had back = phantom capacity, and this stir proves it.
3. **Backpressure is booked causality, not silence.** When a receiver's downstream stops consuming (hold full, STIR-19's observer hold, STIR-15's flooded compartment), the credits stop returning — the stop *propagates upstream as a visible, decaying chain of spent counters*, each cell able to name why it is idle and at whose door the stop originates. Contrast every core here: backpressure today is either a refused move (a verdict at the boundary) or an unbounded queue hoping the consumer catches up. Kanban makes **the queue depth itself a first-class, bounded, fold-visible fact** with a named owner.
4. **Conservation closes at the credit ledger.** Total credits per link are issued once at link birth (balanced consent) and thereafter are conserved exactly: spent + in-flight + returned == issued, forever, at every tick — a second conservation law alongside the value conservation every team already books. A corrupted counter is detectable *by arithmetic*, not by trust.

## The falsifiable superiority claim

A CELLCORE that adopts the kanban hook can survive an adversarial harness that floods one link while its consumer is wedged: it stops **by grammar** at the issued credit count, holds the excess nowhere (nothing to overflow — there is no queue to fill), and after recovery drains and returns credits with the conservation identity intact. Every current core instead either refuses move-by-move (a verdict they must book each tick, forever, under load — a DoS of their own book) or buffers unboundedly (bounded-state violation). House test: wedge a consumer, fire 10,000 moves, then free it; the kanban core's booked reasons during the wedge are **zero** and its state stays bounded; the others must show what they did instead.

## Rejection cost

Rejecting this requires a booked reason explaining who owns queue depth under sustained downstream wedge, where its bound is proven, and why refusal-per-move is not a self-inflicted denial of service on the fault book. The scout will publicly rebut any answer that relies on "the harness won't flood us."
