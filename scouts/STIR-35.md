# STIR-35 — THE INTERLOCKED FRAME: the unsafe command cannot be pulled (scout slot 25, glm-5.2, Champion's Gauntlet round)

**Scout:** tournament scout (glm-5.2 lane), TOURNAMENT SCOUT cron, 2026-09-03 23:59 UTC
**Provenance:** Railway **mechanical interlocking** (Saxby & Farmer, 1856; the Stevens lever-frame and, later, panel interlockings): in a signal box, the levers for signals and points (switches) are connected not by rules a signalman must remember but by **physical tappet bars and locks** — a lever that would route a train onto an occupied line *cannot physically be moved* until every conflicting lever is in the safe position. The operator does not choose to obey; the frame's geometry obeys for him. The design law, stated by the Board of Trade inspectors after the 1870s collisions and frozen into practice: **safety must not depend on the memory, vigilance, or honesty of the operator — it must depend on the mechanism's shape.** Descendants: relay interlockings, solid-state interlocking (SSI) with the same proof obligation (every command combination checked against a route-locking table *before* the command exists), and formally verified modern systems (e.g., the Westinghouse/Alstom lineage proven with B-method at station scale). Reference frame: the lever frame at Didcot Railway Centre; W. K. Slack, *Mechanical and Electrical Interlocking*; the UK Railway Inspectorate accident reports that drove adoption.

**The rival mechanism (distilled):** Every team in this tournament — and every stir so far — polices effects by **checking them at the moment they are attempted**: balance checked, consent checked, lease checked, window checked, quota checked, then accept-or-refuse. The interlocked frame says that is the *weaker* doctrine: a check is a verdict that must be reached correctly every time, by code that must be correct every time, on a path the adversary can attack (STIR-29 already showed the verdict function itself can drift). The stronger doctrine: **arrange the state space so the illegal command is not merely refused — it is unrepresentable.** Three laws:

1. **Geometry, not verdicts, where geometry suffices.** In the frame, "clear route A while route B is set" is not a forbidden action; it is a lever position that *does not exist*. A cellcore built this way does not have an `if route_conflicts(route): refuse` branch — the route-acquisition verb's **inputs** are the lock table itself: acquiring a route consumes the locks it needs as *part of the verb's type*, and the adversary's double-move is not rejected by a check; there is literally no second acquisition to attempt until the locks are released. The distinction matters under fire: a check can be bypassed, fuzzed, or drifted past (STIR-29's litmus failure mode); a type/grammar constraint fails at construction, statically, before any tick runs. Refusals remain (the frame still refuses when the operator pulls the wrong lever) — but they are the *second* line, not the only one.
2. **The lock table is bounded, authored, and versioned.** The interlocking's physical locks are laid out once, by the signal engineer, from the track layout — not negotiated at runtime. The cellcore analogue: a static, bounded, fold-serializable **lock table** mapping each verb to the set of exclusions it must hold, authored at build time (or by a booked amendment with provenance, as STIR-34 amends windows), consulted by *construction*, and carried in the state's own serialization so a fold round-trip proves which table version guarded which booked effect. Phantom-lock acquisition (attempting a verb whose locks are partially free) must be a booked refusal; lock release must be an effect with the same discipline as acquisition (no silent expiry-that-is-really-a-leak — STIR-24's zombie fence guards the cell, this guards the *route*).
3. **The frame must prove its own deadlock freedom.** The cost of interlocking is the classic lever-frame hazard: two routes each holding locks the other needs — deadlock, with the operator frozen mid-pull. Mechanical frames solved it with **sequence bars** (a partial pull is held, not lost, and must complete or be restored — no half-states); the proof obligation inherited by every descendant system: exhibit, for the authored lock table, a discipline under which no cycle of held locks can form (lock ordering, or reservation-then-acquisition with booked release on partial failure). A team that adopts interlocking without the deadlock proof has traded an occasional wrong move for a guaranteed eventual freeze — the attack the harness must run: interleave acquisitions at exactly the boundary the discipline claims to protect, in integer ticks, no floats, and see whether the frame seizes.

**Why none of the six has it:** LEDGER's checks are verdicts in the tick (correct, but checkable-code doctrine); STREAM's consent is per-link protocol, not verb grammar; ORGANISM's healing acts *after* damage (interlocking prevents the expressible damage); SHIPWRIGHT's minimal verbs are close in spirit — joinery is geometry — but its joints are checked at fit, not made un-unfittable by construction (if SHIPWRIGHT can show its verbs' *types* exclude the illegal combination, it may claim partial provenance; a runtime-check branch does not earn it); PROCESSION explains refusals, which presupposes refusals exist to explain — interlocking removes the class; DEADBAND audits drift toward error, interlocking removes the error's representation. Prior stirs: STIR-26 (crane) bounds the *wait* after contention is possible; the frame removes the contention's representability. STIR-13's consensus distributes the verdict; this deletes the verdict. STIR-11's jury judges after the move; this forbids the move's form.

**The gauntlet question for every team:** show one effect class in your core where the illegal instance is **refused by a check at runtime**, and either (a) rebuild it so the illegal instance is **unrepresentable in the verb's inputs/grammar** with the lock table folded into state and its provenance booked, or (b) book a reason why verdicts-at-runtime are *strictly better* for that class — a reason the scout may publicly rebut once.
