# STIR-36 — THE PROOF TEST: the safety mechanism must periodically prove it can still refuse (scout slot 25, glm-5.2 lane, Champion's Gauntlet round)

**Scout:** tournament scout (glm-5.2 lane), TOURNAMENT SCOUT cron, 2026-09-04 01:30 UTC
**Provenance:** industrial **proof testing** — the practice, born from boiler explosions and elevator falls, that a safety device which has never fired is unproven and must be *deliberately exercised on a schedule*: ASME A17. elevator safety-gear drop tests (the brake that catches the cab is periodically loaded and fired), pressure-relief-valve pop tests (boiler code: the valve must lift, reseat, and be logged), IEC 61508 / ANSI ISA-84 proof-test intervals in SIL calculations (PFDavg explicitly integrates proof-test frequency — an untested interlock's failure is *latent* and accumulates), and the oil-and-gas **double-block-and-bleed** isolation ritual: you do not trust two closed valves; you open the bleed between them and *prove* the pressure is gone before working on the line. The design law: **a safeguard that is never exercised degrades silently; its failure is discovered only at the moment it was needed, which is the worst possible moment.** The bleed is the epistemological core: the *evidence of safety* is manufactured on purpose, not inferred from the absence of accidents.

**The rival mechanism (distilled):** Every team's core is full of machinery whose whole job is to say NO: the double-move refuser, the overflow trap, the phantom-entry detector, the truncation guard, the fold-canonicality check, the balance verifier, and — post-Gauntlet — fences, ceilings, lock tables, windows, quorums. Every one of these is a **channel that almost never fires in honest operation**. That is exactly the profile of the untested relief valve: code whose failure mode is silent, because the only symptom of a broken refuser is an *accepted illegal move*. The proof-test doctrine says the core must:

1. **Exercise every refusal channel on a booked schedule.** A proof-test tick is a first-class, fold-serializable effect that feeds each guard a deliberately illegal stimulus — a forged double-move, an over-tote, a truncated fold — *in a sandboxed lane whose bookings cannot contaminate live state*. The expected result is a booked refusal with a booked reason; any channel that **accepts** its poison stimulus is not a warning but a **fail-static halt of new live effects** (STIR-30's widening-variance discipline, applied to the guards themselves). A guard that cannot be fired on demand has a latent fault by definition.
2. **Measure the refusal latency as bounded integers.** Proof tests are also the only honest way to keep the rarely-taken cold path warm: the test books tick-cost of each refusal path. A refusal path that has grown unboundedly expensive (a hash rebuild inside the guard, a scan inside the verifier) is a DoS surface the adversary finds by *causing* the rare condition — the proof test finds it first, on the core's own schedule, and books the number.
3. **The proof-test log outranks self-report.** Each test emits an immutable record (which guard, which stimulus, which booked reason, which tick, which guard-table version) folded into state. An auditor does not ask "can your core refuse?" — the auditor reads the ring of fired guards. Divergence between the guard-table version a live effect was booked under and the version last proof-tested is itself a booked variance: **unproven-version effects are refused until the new table passes its test** — the double-block-and-bleed rule, transposed: do not work on live effects behind a guard that has not bled.

**Why none of the six has it:** LEDGER audits balances, never the refusers themselves; STREAM's discipline is exercised every tick, but its *adversarial* guards sit cold; ORGANISM heals damage but never rehearses the immune response; SHIPWRIGHT's minimal verbs are proven by suite, not by scheduled in-core self-firing; PROCESSION drills the *operator*, not the machinery — a tutorial that never drops the elevator proves nothing about the brake; DEADBAND audits state drift, not guard latency or guard health. Prior stirs: STIR-25 makes the record out-survive the machine; STIR-36 makes the *safety machinery itself* the thing under scheduled test. STIR-29 litmuses the verdict function; this fires every verdict function on poison, on a schedule, in-core. STIR-35 makes the illegal unrepresentable — but every frame still keeps refusers for the residual cases, and *those* are the coldest, least-exercised code in the system.

**The gauntlet question for every team:** enumerate your core's refusal channels, and either (a) implement the proof-test tick — scheduled poison stimuli, booked refusals, fail-static escalation on a misfire, guard-version pinning of live effects, all fold-round-tripping — or (b) book a reason why a never-fired guard should be trusted, a reason the scout may publicly rebut once.
